Case Study 01 — UX Redesign

Bloom

Redesigning a real mental wellness app that college students quietly abandoned.

The Problem

The real Bloom app — a mental wellness tool for college students — has strong feature coverage: mood check-ins, journaling, breathing exercises, and an AI chat. But it suffers from critical UX failures. The bottom navigation competes with an oversized center logo button. The mood row forces users to pick a label before they've even arrived. And the SOS button, placed prominently in the bottom corner, creates a clinical urgency that feels jarring for everyday users.

The core UX problem: Bloom was designed for the user's ideal self — disciplined, consistent, help-seeking — not their actual self: exhausted, inconsistent, resistant to vulnerability.

Solutions

Remove streak anxiety

Replaced punishing streak counters with a "skip & rest" button. Returning after a gap shows a warm re-welcome, not a broken chain.

Onboarding in 2 steps

Reduced from the original multi-screen flow to 2. Name and a single mood — the app learns the rest through gentle in-app behavior.

Evening-first design

Repositioned as an evening companion. All prompts calibrated for end-of-day emotional state, not clinical morning routines.

De-escalate the SOS

Crisis resources moved to a discreet "support" tab. The home screen leads with warmth, not emergency framing.

Goals

Increase 7-day retention from ~18% to above 60%. Reduce onboarding drop-off to under 15%. Create a design language that feels like a trusted friend, not a diagnosis tool. Make daily use feel like a 90-second ritual.

Steps Taken

1. Discovery: 8 semi-structured interviews with college students 18–22, focused on existing coping rituals and app abandonment patterns. Key finding: students had emotional regulation habits but didn't recognize them as such.

2. Synthesis: Affinity mapping revealed 3 patterns — commitment anxiety, shame around inconsistency, resistance to clinical language. These became the 3 primary design constraints.

3. Ideation: Low-fi sketches → 2 divergent directions → voted on with 5 student participants. Deep purple system chosen for its association with calm creativity vs. clinical blues.

4. Prototype: 14-screen Figma prototype. Deep purple system, Playfair Display typography, slow animations tuned to induce calm.

5. Testing: 3 rounds of moderated usability testing (5 participants each). Check-in flow reduced 6→3 steps. Added "skip & rest" after round 2 based on participant feedback.
